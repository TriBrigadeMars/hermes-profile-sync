# Qualitative Review Manuscript Structure

Standard sections for the synthesis manuscript. Each section maps to specific cross-cutting themes.

## Section-to-Theme Mapping

| Manuscript Section | Informed By |
|--------------------|-------------|
| Introduction | Prevalence data from articles; scope of the problem |
| Theoretical Frameworks | Frameworks cited across multiple articles |
| Current Landscape | Policy/regulatory articles; compliance studies |
| What Works | Themes with positive evidence (programs, interventions) |
| Persistent Gaps | Themes with negative evidence (barriers, failures) |
| Recommendations | Synthesis of "What Works" + "Gaps" → actionable steps |

## Writing Tips

- **Abstract**: Write LAST. Summarize the entire manuscript in 200-300 words.
- **Introduction**: Start broad (national scope), narrow to the specific review question.
- **Theoretical Frameworks**: Pick 2-3 that appear across multiple articles. Don't force frameworks that only appear in one source.
- **What Works**: Name specific programs with effect sizes. "Safe Dates produced 56-92% reductions" is stronger than "programs showed positive results."
- **Persistent Gaps**: Each gap should cite 2-3 articles. Don't list gaps without evidence.
- **Recommendations**: Number them. Each should cite at least one supporting study. Make them actionable (start with a verb).
- **Conclusion**: Return to the opening paradox. End with a call to action.
- **References**: APA 7 format. Include ALL cited articles from the ingested batches plus any supplementary sources.
