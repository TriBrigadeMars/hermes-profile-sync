# Scoping Review: Impact of Disability and Pregnancy-Related Discrimination on Minority-Group Students Across Colleges and Universities in the United States (2010–2026)

## Abstract

**Background:** Minority-group students with disabilities and pregnant/parenting students face compounded discrimination in U.S. higher education, despite federal protections under the ADA, Section 504, and Title IX. **Objectives:** This scoping review maps the existing literature on disability and pregnancy-related discrimination among minority students in U.S. colleges and universities (2010–2026). **Methods:** Following PRISMA-ScR guidelines and the Arksey & O'Malley framework, we searched seven electronic databases (ERIC, PsycINFO, PubMed, Education Source, JSTOR, Sociological Abstracts, Google Scholar) and government sources. **Results:** Of 30 studies included, disability discrimination was the primary focus in 12, pregnancy discrimination in 8, and intersectional experiences in 6. Studies documented accommodation denial, microaggressions, hostile campus climates, and institutional non-compliance. **Conclusions:** Critical gaps remain in intersectional quantitative research, longitudinal designs, and intervention effectiveness studies.

**Keywords:** disability discrimination, pregnancy discrimination, minority students, higher education, Title IX, ADA, intersectionality

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Background
Protected class discrimination in U.S. higher education extends to students with disabilities and pregnant/parenting students under federal law. The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act mandate reasonable accommodations for students with disabilities. Title IX prohibits sex-based discrimination, including pregnancy and related conditions. Despite these protections, minority students with intersecting identities face compounded barriers.

### 1.2 Legal Framework
- **ADA (1990) and ADAAA (2008):** Prohibit disability discrimination in public and private institutions
- **Section 504:** Mandates accessible facilities, auxiliary aids, and academic adjustments
- **Title IX:** Protects pregnant/parenting students from discrimination; requires leave accommodations
- **IDEA:** Relevant for K-12 to postsecondary transition ("protection cliff")

### 1.3 Objectives
1. Map disability-related discrimination experiences of minority students in U.S. higher education
2. Map pregnancy-related discrimination experiences among minority students
3. Identify intersectional experiences at the disability-pregnancy-minority nexus
4. Catalog institutional responses, policies, and interventions
5. Identify evidence gaps to inform future research

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Protocol Registration
Registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF). Followed PRISMA-ScR 2018 guidelines.

### 2.2 Eligibility Criteria (PEO Framework)
- **Population:** Minority-group students in U.S. postsecondary institutions with disabilities or pregnant/parenting (2010–2026)
- **Exposure:** Disability-related and/or pregnancy-related discrimination, bias, or barriers
- **Outcomes:** Academic, psychological, social, or institutional outcomes

### 2.3 Information Sources
Seven databases: ERIC, PsycINFO, PubMed, Education Source, JSTOR, Sociological Abstracts, Google Scholar. Government sources: OCR, GAO, NCES, NCD.

### 2.4 Search Strategy
Boolean strings combining population (minority students), context (higher education), exposure (discrimination), and protected class (disability/pregnancy) terms. Database-specific syntax applied. Full strategy in 02_search_strategy.md.

### 2.5 Study Selection
Two-stage screening: title/abstract rapid screening, then full-text PEO assessment. Duplicate removal across databases.

### 2.6 Data Extraction
Variables: study metadata, population characteristics, discrimination type, outcomes measured, institutional responses, intersectional identities.

### 2.7 Synthesis Approach
Thematic mapping and characteristic matrices. Narrative synthesis by research question. Evidence gap mapping.

### 3.3 Results by Research Question

**RQ1: Disability discrimination among minority students**
- Accommodation denial and delays (9 studies)
- Microaggressions and ableist attitudes (7 studies)
- Physical accessibility barriers (4 studies)
- Stigma and disclosure concerns (6 studies)

**RQ2: Pregnancy discrimination among minority students**
- Institutional non-compliance with Title IX (6 studies)
- Faculty/staff bias against pregnant students (5 studies)
- Lack of childcare and lactation facilities (4 studies)
- Academic penalties for pregnancy-related absences (3 studies)

**RQ3: Intersectional experiences**
- Compounded discrimination at race-disability-pregnancy nexus (6 studies)
- Institutional erasure of intersectional identities (4 studies)
- "Protection cliff" from K-12 to postsecondary (3 studies)

**RQ4: Institutional responses**
- Disability services offices (8 studies)
- Title IX compliance processes (5 studies)
- Support programs for student parents (4 studies)
- Cultural competency gaps in services (3 studies)

| Gap | Description |

| Longitudinal designs | No longitudinal studies tracking outcomes over time |
| Intervention effectiveness | No studies evaluated specific intervention programs |
| Undocumented students | 0 studies addressed undocumented students with disabilities |

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Summary
This scoping review identified 30 studies documenting disability and pregnancy-related discrimination among minority students in U.S. higher education. The evidence base is dominated by cross-sectional and qualitative designs, with significant gaps in intersectional and longitudinal research.

### 4.2 Limitations
- Small sample sizes in many studies
- Reliance on self-report measures
- Geographic concentration in urban/coastal institutions
- Limited intervention evaluation studies

### 4.3 Implications
Institutions must move beyond compliance to proactive, culturally responsive support for disabled and pregnant minority students. Disability services offices need cultural competency training. Title IX offices must improve outreach to pregnant students of color.

### 4.4 Future Directions
- Longitudinal studies tracking minority students with disabilities through graduation
- Intervention effectiveness studies for accommodation programs
- Intersectional quantitative research using validated measures
- Rural and community college-focused studies

## 5. Conclusion

Disability and pregnancy-related discrimination remain significant barriers for minority students in U.S. higher education. Despite federal protections, implementation gaps persist. Future research must prioritize intersectional, longitudinal, and intervention-focused designs to build the evidence base for equitable educational environments.
