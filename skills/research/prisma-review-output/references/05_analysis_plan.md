# Analysis Plan — Scoping Review

## Overview
This analysis plan follows the JBI scoping review methodology (Peters et al., 2020) and PRISMA-ScR guidelines (Tricco et al., 2018). Given that scoping reviews map evidence rather than synthesize effect sizes, the analysis is primarily descriptive and thematic rather than meta-analytic.

---

## 2. Thematic Analysis

### 2.1 Approach
A six-phase thematic analysis approach (Braun & Clarke, 2006) will be used to identify, analyze, and report patterns across included studies:

1. **Familiarization**: Read and re-read extracted data from included studies
2. **Initial coding**: Systematically code interesting features across the entire dataset
3. **Theme generation**: Collate codes into potential themes
4. **Theme review**: Check themes against coded extracts and the full dataset
5. **Theme definition**: Refine and name each theme
6. **Report production**: Select vivid, compelling extract examples; final analysis

### 2.2 A Priori Thematic Framework
Based on the PEO framework and literature review, the following a priori themes will be used as a starting coding framework:

#### Theme Cluster 1: Nature of Discrimination
- Direct discrimination (denial of services, exclusion)
- Indirect discrimination (inflexible policies, inaccessible environments)
- Systemic/institutional discrimination
- Microaggressions and subtle bias
- Intersectional discrimination (compounded disadvantage)

#### Theme Cluster 2: Institutional Factors
- Disability services and accommodation processes
- Title IX compliance and enforcement
- Campus climate and culture
- Institutional type (HBCU, HSI, PWI, community college)
- Resource allocation and funding

#### Theme Cluster 3: Student Experiences and Outcomes
- Academic persistence and performance
- Psychological well-being and mental health
- Social integration and belonging
- Self-advocacy and agency
- Help-seeking behaviors

#### Theme Cluster 4: Intersecting Identities
- Disability × race interactions
- Pregnancy × race interactions
- Gender × disability × race
- Socioeconomic status as modifier
- Immigration status as modifier

#### Theme Cluster 5: Coping and Resilience
- Student-led support networks
- Community-based resources
- Institutional allies
- Self-advocacy strategies
- Resilience and empowerment

#### Theme Cluster 6: Policy and Practice
- ADA/Section 504 compliance gaps
- Title IX enforcement challenges
- Best practices and model programs
- Recommendations for institutions
- Policy reform proposals

### 2.3 Inductive Coding
Emergent themes not captured by the a priori framework will be identified through inductive coding. Both approaches will be integrated in the final thematic map.

## 3. Evidence Gap Map

### 3.1 Structure
An evidence gap map (EGM) will be created as a matrix:

**Rows**: Outcome domains (academic, psychological, social, institutional)
**Columns**: Population intersections (disability × minority, pregnancy × minority, disability × pregnancy × minority)

Each cell will indicate:
- Number of studies (size)
- Study designs represented (color)
- Quality distribution (shading)

### 3.2 Identification of Gaps
The EGM will explicitly identify:
- Populations understudied (cells with few/no studies)
- Outcome domains lacking evidence for specific intersections
- Methodological gaps (e.g., lack of longitudinal data)
- Geographic gaps (regions with no studies)
- Theoretical gaps (underused frameworks)

## 4. Evidence Mapping Visualizations

### 4.1 Required Visualizations
1. **PRISMA-ScR Flow Diagram**: Records identified → screened → included
2. **Publication Timeline**: Bar chart of studies by year
3. **Study Design Distribution**: Pie/donut chart
4. **Geographic Map**: U.S. map showing study locations
5. **Evidence Gap Map**: Heat map of outcomes × intersections
6. **Theoretical Framework Treemap**: Size by frequency
7. **Quality Distribution**: Stacked bar chart by study design
8. **Discrimination Type Network**: Co-occurrence of discrimination types
9. **Outcome Domain Sankey Diagram**: Flow from discrimination type → outcome domain

## 5. Narrative Synthesis

Beyond the numerical summary, a narrative synthesis will:
- Describe the overall body of evidence
- Highlight key findings across studies
- Identify consistencies and contradictions
- Discuss the state of intersectional research
- Articulate policy implications
- Identify priority areas for future research
