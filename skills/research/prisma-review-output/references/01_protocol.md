## 1. Background and Rationale

### 1.1 Context
Discrimination against students with disabilities and pregnant/parenting students in U.S. higher education represents a persistent and multifaceted challenge. Despite robust federal protections, research consistently documents that these students face barriers to full academic participation, with effects compounded when students also belong to racial, ethnic, or other minority groups.

### 1.2 Legal Framework

#### Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) of 1990 and ADA Amendments Act (ADAAA) of 2008
The ADA prohibits discrimination on the basis of disability in all public and private institutions that receive federal financial assistance. Title III extends protections to private colleges and universities. The ADAAA broadened the definition of disability, making it easier for individuals to establish eligibility for protections. Under the ADA, institutions must provide reasonable accommodations and cannot exclude qualified individuals with disabilities from participation in or benefits of services, programs, or activities.

#### Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973
Section 504 prohibits discrimination against individuals with disabilities in any program or activity receiving federal financial assistance. In higher education, this mandates that institutions provide accessible facilities, auxiliary aids, and academic adjustments. Students with disabilities are entitled to reasonable accommodations including extended testing time, note-taking assistance, accessible housing, and modified course requirements.

#### Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972
Title IX prohibits sex-based discrimination in federally funded education programs. The 2020 Title IX regulations under the Trump administration and subsequent 2024 Biden administration proposed rules expanded protections. Critically, Title IX's protections encompass pregnancy and related conditions:
- Institutions cannot discriminate against students based on pregnancy, childbirth, false pregnancy, termination of pregnancy, or recovery therefrom
- Schools must provide leave of absence for pregnancy-related conditions
- Institutions must ensure pregnant students can participate in classes and extracurricular activities
- Students returning from pregnancy-related leave must be reinstated to their academic and extracurricular status

#### Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA)
While primarily applicable to K-12 education, IDEA is relevant to this review because students transitioning from IDEA-protected environments to higher education under ADA/Section 504 may experience a "protection cliff" where the proactive, individualized support model of IDEA gives way to the more self-advocacy-dependent model of postsecondary disability services.

### 1.3 Intersectionality and Compounded Discrimination
Intersectionality theory (Crenshaw, 1989) provides the critical lens for this review. Minority-group students who also have disabilities or who are pregnant/parenting may experience:
- **Double jeopardy**: Compounded disadvantage from simultaneous membership in multiple marginalized groups
- **Intersectional invisibility**: Protocols and services designed for one dimension of identity may fail to address intersectional needs
- **Institutional erasure**: Data systems and support structures may not capture or serve intersectional identities

### 1.4 Gaps in the Literature
While substantial literature exists on disability discrimination in higher education and on Title IX pregnancy protections, less is known about:
- How these forms of discrimination intersect with racial/ethnic minority status
- Institutional variation in compliance and support
- Psychological and academic outcomes at the intersection of disability, pregnancy, and minority status
- The lived experiences of students navigating multiple marginalized identities

## 2. Review Questions

### Primary Research Questions
1. **RQ1**: What is the nature and extent of disability-related discrimination experienced by minority-group students in U.S. colleges and universities between 2010 and 2026?
2. **RQ2**: What is the nature and extent of pregnancy-related discrimination experienced by minority-group students in U.S. colleges and universities between 2010 and 2026?
3. **RQ3**: How do disability and pregnancy-related discrimination intersect to affect the academic, psychological, social, and institutional outcomes of minority-group students?

### Secondary Research Questions
4. **RQ4**: What institutional policies, programs, or practices have been implemented to address disability and/or pregnancy-related discrimination among minority students in higher education?
5. **RQ5**: What theoretical frameworks have been used to study the intersection of disability, pregnancy, and minority status in higher education?

## 3. PEO Framework

This scoping review uses the **Population-Exposure-Outcomes (PEO)** framework, which is particularly suited to reviews examining risk factors or exposures rather than interventions (Moola et al., 2020).

### 3.1 Population (P)
**Minority-group students enrolled in U.S. colleges and universities (2010–2026) who:**
- Have documented or undocumented disabilities (physical, cognitive, learning, psychiatric, sensory, chronic health conditions)
- Are pregnant, have recently given birth, are parenting, or have experienced pregnancy-related conditions (including false pregnancy, termination of pregnancy, miscarriage)
- Identify as racial/ethnic minorities (including but not limited to Black/African American, Hispanic/Latino/a/x, Native American/Alaska Native, Asian American, Pacific Islander, multiracial)
- May also include other minority statuses (LGBTQ+, first-generation college students, undocumented students) when intersecting with disability or pregnancy

**Inclusion criteria for population:**
- Enrolled in 2-year or 4-year postsecondary institutions in the United States
- Any age, gender, or enrollment status (full-time, part-time)
- Undergraduate or graduate level

### 3.2 Exposure (E)
**Disability-related and/or pregnancy-related discrimination, including:**
- Direct discrimination (denial of accommodations, harassment, exclusion from programs/activities)
- Indirect discrimination (inaccessible environments, inflexible policies that disproportionately affect disabled/pregnant students)
- Systemic/institutional discrimination (failure to provide mandated services, hostile campus climate)
- Microaggressions and subtle forms of discrimination
- Intersectional discrimination (compounded effects of multiple marginalized identities)

**Measures of exposure may include:**
- Self-reported discrimination experiences
- Documented complaints (OCR, EEOC, institutional)
- Accommodation request data
- Climate survey results
- Legal case documentation

### 3.3 Outcomes (O)

#### Academic Outcomes
- GPA and academic performance
- Course completion and persistence
- Graduation/completion rates
- Time to degree
- Academic probation and dismissal rates
- Transfer patterns (including stop-out and dropout)

#### Psychological Outcomes
- Mental health (depression, anxiety, stress, PTSD)
- Self-efficacy and self-advocacy
- Sense of belonging and campus integration
- Academic identity and self-concept
- Help-seeking behavior
- Resilience and coping

#### Social Outcomes
- Peer relationships and social support
- Faculty/staff interactions
- Campus climate perceptions
- Extracurricular participation
- Social isolation and marginalization

#### Institutional Outcomes
- Accommodation utilization and satisfaction
- Institutional complaint processes
- Policy compliance (ADA, Section 504, Title IX)
- Support service availability and accessibility
- Institutional response to discrimination reports

## 4. Eligibility Criteria

### 4.1 Inclusion Criteria
| Criterion | Specification |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Population** | Minority-group students (racial/ethnic, gender, LGBTQ+, first-generation, or other minority status) enrolled in U.S. postsecondary institutions who have disabilities or are pregnant/parenting |
| **Exposure** | Experience of disability-related and/or pregnancy-related discrimination, bias, or barriers |
| **Outcomes** | Academic, psychological, social, or institutional outcomes related to discrimination |
| **Study design** | Quantitative (survey, experimental, quasi-experimental, observational), qualitative (interview, focus group, ethnographic, case study), or mixed-methods |
| **Setting** | 2-year colleges, 4-year colleges and universities, graduate/professional schools in the United States |
| **Time frame** | Published between January 2010 and August 2026 |
| **Language** | English-language publications |
| **Publication type** | Peer-reviewed journal articles, dissertations/theses, government reports, institutional reports, conference proceedings with full text available |

### 4.2 Exclusion Criteria
| Criterion | Rationale |
|-----------|-----------|
| Studies conducted outside the United States | Different legal frameworks and institutional contexts |
| Studies focusing exclusively on K-12 settings | Different legal protections (IDEA vs. ADA/Section 504) |
| Studies not addressing minority-group populations | Focus of review on intersectionality |
| Studies published before 2010 | Ensuring relevance to current legal/policy landscape |
| Non-English publications | Resource constraints |
| Opinion pieces, editorials, letters without empirical data | Insufficient rigor |
| Studies focusing exclusively on faculty/staff | Population mismatch |
| Studies on disability or pregnancy without discrimination/outcomes component | Exposure/outcome mismatch |

## 6. Search Strategy Overview

Boolean search strings will be developed for each database using a combination of:
- **Population terms**: minority students, racial/ethnic minorities, students of color, underrepresented students, Black students, Hispanic students, etc.
- **Exposure terms**: discrimination, bias, harassment, barriers, hostile climate, inequity
- **Context terms**: higher education, college, university, postsecondary
- **Disability terms**: disability, disabled, accommodations, ADA, Section 504, learning disability, physical disability, mental health
- **Pregnancy terms**: pregnancy, pregnant, parenting, Title IX, maternity, motherhood

Search strings are detailed in `02_search_strategy.md`.
