# Quality Assessment and Risk of Bias Framework

## Overview
This document outlines the quality assessment framework for the scoping review on disability and pregnancy-related discrimination among minority students in U.S. higher education. Given the expected heterogeneity of study designs (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods), multiple quality assessment tools are specified.

---

## 1. Quality Assessment Tools by Study Design

### 1.1 Quantitative Studies — JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Analytical Cross-Sectional Studies

| # | Criterion | Guiding Questions |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Criteria for inclusion | Were the criteria for inclusion in the sample clearly defined? |
| 2 | Study subjects and setting | Were the study subjects and the setting described in detail? |
| 3 | Exposure measurement | Was the exposure measured in a valid and reliable way? |
| 4 | Standard condition | Were objective, standard criteria used for measurement of the condition? |
| 5 | Confounding factors | Were confounding factors identified? |
| 6 | Confounding strategies | Were strategies to deal with confounding factors stated? |
| 7 | Outcome measurement | Were the outcomes measured in a valid and reliable way? |
| 8 | Appropriate statistics | Was appropriate statistical analysis used? |

**Scoring**: Yes / No / Unclear / Not Applicable
**Quality thresholds**: High (≥7/8 Yes), Moderate (5-6/8 Yes), Low (≤4/8 Yes)

### 1.2 Qualitative Studies — JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Qualitative Research

| # | Criterion | Guiding Questions |
|---|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Congruity methodology | Is there congruity between the stated philosophical perspective and the research methodology? |
| 2 | Congruity methods | Is there congruity between the research methodology and the research question or objectives? |
| 3 | Congruity data collection | Is there congruity between the research methodology and the methods of data collection? |
| 4 | Congruity representation | Is there congruity between the research methodology and the representation and analysis of data? |
| 5 | Congruity interpretation | Is there congruity between the research methodology and the interpretation of results? |
| 6 | Paradigm influence | Is there a statement locating the researcher culturally or theoretically? |
| 7 | Influence on research | Is the influence of the researcher on the research, and vice-versa, addressed? |
| 8 | Participants articulated | Are participants and their voices adequately represented? |
| 9 | Ethical approval | Is the research ethical according to current criteria or evidence of ethical approval? |
| 10 | Conclusions drawn | Do the conclusions drawn flow from the analysis or interpretation of the data? |

**Scoring**: Yes / No / Unclear / Not Applicable
**Quality thresholds**: High (≥8/10 Yes), Moderate (6-7/10 Yes), Low (≤5/10 Yes)

### 1.3 Mixed-Methods Studies — JBI Critical Appraisal Checklist for Mixed Methods

Assess the quantitative and qualitative components separately using the above tools, plus:

| # | Criterion |
|---|-----------|
| 1 | Is there an adequate rationale for using a mixed-methods design? |
| 2 | Are the different components of the study effectively integrated? |
| 3 | Is the mixed-methods design appropriate for addressing the research question? |
| 4 | Is the output of the integration of qualitative and quantitative components adequately interpreted? |
| 5 | Are the limitations associated with the mixed-methods design addressed? |

### 1.4 Secondary Data Analysis Studies
| # | Criterion |
|---|-----------|
| 1 | Was the data source clearly described? |
| 2 | Were the variables relevant to the research question? |
| 3 | Was the analytic sample appropriate? |
| 4 | Were inclusion/exclusion criteria clearly stated? |
| 5 | Were missing data handled appropriately? |
| 6 | Were complex survey designs (if applicable) accounted for? |
| 7 | Were appropriate analytic methods used? |
| 8 | Were results interpreted in the context of the original data source's limitations? |

## 2. Disability-Specific Quality Domains

Beyond standard quality assessment tools, reviewers should evaluate each study on these disability-specific quality domains:

### 2.1 Accommodation Context
- Does the study describe the accommodation request/approval process?
- Are the types of accommodations studied specified?
- Does the study distinguish between different disability types?

### 2.2 Intersectionality Rigor
- Does the study explicitly address intersectionality in its design?
- Are intersecting identities analyzed separately or jointly (not collapsed)?
- Does the study avoid treating disability or race as monolithic categories?

### 2.3 Disability Model
- Does the study use a disability model (medical, social, human rights, biopsychosocial)?
- Is the chosen model appropriate for the research question?
- Does the study acknowledge the difference between impairment and disability?

### 2.4 Voice and Agency
- Are students with disabilities and/or pregnant/parenting students involved in research design?
- Are participant voices centered in qualitative findings?
- Does the study avoid deficit framing?

### 2.5 Structural Context
- Does the study describe the institutional context (size, type, resources)?
- Are relevant institutional policies described?
- Does the study consider the legal context (ADA, Section 504, Title IX)?

---

## 3. Pregnancy-Specific Quality Domains

### 3.1 Pregnancy Status Specification
- Is pregnancy status clearly defined (currently pregnant, recently gave birth, parenting)?
- Does the study distinguish between pregnancy, parenting, and caregiving?
- Are pregnancy-related conditions (e.g., bedrest, complications) addressed?

### 3.2 Title IX Context
- Does the study discuss Title IX protections for pregnant/parenting students?
- Are institutional Title IX compliance mechanisms described?
- Does the study address the 2020 or 2024 Title IX regulatory changes?

### 3.3 Gendered Dimensions
- Does the study consider how pregnancy discrimination differs from general gender discrimination?
- Are intersectional gendered experiences examined (e.g., pregnant women of color)?

## 5. Overall Quality Rating

### Rating Algorithm
1. **Standard quality score**: Based on applicable JBI checklist (quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods)
2. **Disability-specific score**: 0-5 based on five disability domains
3. **Pregnancy-specific score**: 0-3 based on three pregnancy domains (N/A if not applicable)

### Final Quality Categories
| Category | Criteria |
|----------|----------|
| **High quality** | Standard score ≥7/8 or ≥8/10; disability-specific ≥3/5; pregnancy-specific ≥2/3 (if applicable) |
| **Moderate quality** | Standard score 5-6/8 or 6-7/10; disability-specific ≥2/5; pregnancy-specific ≥1/3 |
| **Low quality** | Standard score ≤4/8 or ≤5/10; OR disability-specific <2/5; OR pregnancy-specific <1/3 |

---

## 6. Quality Assessment Documentation

For each study, document:
1. **Tool applied**: Which JBI/MMAT checklist was used
2. **Individual ratings**: Score for each criterion
3. **Rationale**: Supporting evidence for each rating (quote from study when possible)
4. **Disability-specific ratings**: Scores for the five disability domains
5. **Pregnancy-specific ratings**: Scores for the three pregnancy domains (N/A if not applicable)
6. **Overall quality rating**: High / Moderate / Low
7. **Key quality concerns**: Specific limitations identified
8. **Impact on findings**: How quality concerns may affect the reliability of findings

---

## 7. Inter-Rater Reliability

- Two reviewers independently assess quality for each included study
- Calculate Cohen's kappa for overall quality ratings
- Target: κ ≥ 0.70 (substantial agreement)
- Discrepancies resolved through discussion with methodologist if needed
