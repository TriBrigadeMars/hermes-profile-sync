## 1. Background and Rationale

Sexual harassment and discrimination remain persistent problems in U.S. higher education, disproportionately affecting students from historically marginalized racial, ethnic, gender, and sexual orientation minority groups. Despite decades of federal legislation—including Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act (Clery Act), and the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA)—evidence suggests that minority students continue to experience hostile educational environments at elevated rates.

### 1.1 Legislative and Regulatory Context

**Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972** prohibits sex-based discrimination in any education program or activity receiving federal financial assistance. Title IX has been interpreted to cover sexual harassment, sexual assault, and sex-based discrimination in educational settings. The 2020 Title IX regulations under the Trump administration narrowed the definition of sexual harassment and imposed specific procedural requirements, while the Biden administration's 2024 Final Rule expanded protections to include gender identity and sexual orientation. The shifting regulatory landscape has created institutional uncertainty about compliance obligations and survivor protections.

**Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964** prohibits discrimination based on race, color, or national origin in programs receiving federal financial assistance. Title VI applies to racial harassment, hostile educational environments based on race, and institutional failures to address discriminatory conduct. Intersectional claims—where students experience discrimination based on both race and sex—fall under both Title IX and Title VI.

**The Clery Act (1990, amended 2013)** requires colleges and universities participating in federal financial aid programs to disclose campus crime statistics and security information, including incidents of sexual assault, dating violence, domestic violence, and stalking. The Campus SaVE Act (2013) amendments expanded reporting requirements and mandated institutional training programs.

**The Violence Against Women Act (VAWA), reauthorized in 2013 and 2022**, requires institutions to provide programming on domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, and stalking; to establish disciplinary procedures; and to provide victims' rights notifications. VAWA's campus provisions specifically address the intersection of gender-based violence and educational access.

### 1.2 Conceptual Framework

This review employs a **social-ecological framework** to understand the multilevel factors influencing minority students' experiences of harassment and discrimination in higher education:

- **Individual level**: Student demographics, prior victimization, coping strategies, help-seeking behaviors
- **Interpersonal level**: Peer relationships, faculty-student interactions, power dynamics
- **Institutional level**: Campus climate, reporting mechanisms, administrative responses, diversity infrastructure
- **Community/Societal level**: State policy environments, political climate, cultural norms, media representations

### 1.3 Gap in the Literature

While research on campus sexual assault has grown substantially since 2010 (particularly following the "It's On Us" campaign and increased Title IX enforcement), the intersection of sexual harassment/discrimination with minority status remains underexplored. Existing reviews have focused primarily on prevalence rates without systematically mapping the breadth of outcomes experienced by minority students, the institutional responses deployed, or the effectiveness of prevention and intervention programs for these populations.

## 2. Review Questions

### Primary Research Question
What is the nature and extent of the evidence regarding the impact of sexual harassment and discrimination on minority-group students (racial/ethnic, gender, sexual orientation, disability) in U.S. colleges and universities between 2010 and 2026?

### Secondary Research Questions
1. What are the documented academic, psychological, social, and institutional outcomes of sexual harassment and discrimination for minority students in higher education?
2. How do intersectional identities (e.g., race × gender, race × sexual orientation) shape the experiences and outcomes of sexual harassment and discrimination among minority students?
3. What institutional responses, prevention programs, and policy interventions have been implemented to address sexual harassment and discrimination affecting minority students, and what evidence exists for their effectiveness?
4. What methodological approaches have been used to study sexual harassment and discrimination among minority students in higher education, and what are the identified gaps?

## 3. PEO Framework

### 3.1 Population
**Inclusion**: Students enrolled in U.S. colleges and universities (2-year and 4-year institutions, public and private) who identify as members of one or more minority groups:
- Racial/ethnic minorities (Black/African American, Hispanic/Latinx, Asian American/Pacific Islander, Native American/Alaska Native, multiracial)
- Gender minorities (transgender, nonbinary, gender nonconforming)
- Sexual orientation minorities (lesbian, gay, bisexual, queer, questioning)
- Students with disabilities
- Undocumented students

**Exclusion**: Students at non-U.S. institutions; studies exclusively focused on majority-group students without minority subgroup analysis; K-12 settings; for-profit vocational schools without degree-granting programs.

### 3.2 Exposure
**Inclusion**: Experiences of sexual harassment and/or discrimination in the campus context, including but not limited to:
- Sexual harassment (quid pro quo, hostile environment)
- Sexual assault, rape, attempted rape
- Racial harassment, racial discrimination
- Gender-based harassment, gender discrimination
- Intersectional harassment/discrimination (simultaneous targeting based on multiple identities)
- Online/cyber sexual harassment
- Institutional discrimination (failure to respond, retaliation)
- Microaggressions related to sexual or racial identity in educational settings

**Exclusion**: Experiences occurring entirely off-campus with no connection to educational life; workplace harassment in non-student employment roles; harassment unrelated to protected characteristics.

### 3.3 Outcomes
**Academic Outcomes**:
- GPA changes, academic performance
- Course withdrawal, academic probation
- Degree completion, time to degree
- Academic engagement, class attendance
- Major/field of study changes
- Graduate school aspirations/attainment

**Psychological Outcomes**:
- Depression, anxiety, PTSD symptoms
- Self-esteem, self-efficacy
- Substance use/abuse
- Suicidal ideation, self-harm
- Stress, burnout
- Sense of belonging

**Social Outcomes**:
- Social isolation, withdrawal from campus activities
- Peer relationship quality
- Trust in institutions
- Help-seeking behaviors
- Campus involvement, extracurricular participation

**Institutional Outcomes**:
- Retention, persistence, transfer
- Institutional satisfaction
- Reporting behaviors (formal and informal)
- Engagement with Title IX offices
- Campus climate perceptions

## 4. Eligibility Criteria

### 4.1 Inclusion Criteria
| Criterion | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Population** | Minority students (racial/ethnic, gender, sexual orientation, disability) enrolled in U.S. colleges/universities |
| **Exposure** | Sexual harassment and/or discrimination (including intersectional experiences) |
| **Outcomes** | Academic, psychological, social, and/or institutional outcomes |
| **Study Design** | Quantitative, qualitative, and mixed-methods studies; cross-sectional, longitudinal, experimental, quasi-experimental, case studies, surveys |
| **Time Period** | Published between January 2010 and August 2026 |
| **Language** | English-language publications |
| **Geography** | United States (including territories) |
| **Publication Type** | Peer-reviewed journal articles, dissertations, government reports, institutional reports |

### 4.2 Exclusion Criteria
| Criterion | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Non-U.S. settings** | Studies conducted entirely outside the United States |
| **Non-minority focus** | Studies exclusively examining majority populations without disaggregation for minority subgroups |
| **Non-exposure** | Studies examining discrimination without sexual harassment component, or sexual harassment without discrimination dimensions |
| **Non-outcome** | Editorials, commentaries, opinion pieces, conference abstracts without full data |
| **Pre-2010** | Publications before January 2010 |
| **Non-English** | Non-English publications without English translation |

## 6. Review Design

### 6.1 Methodology
This scoping review will follow the **JBI (Joanna Briggs Institute) methodology for scoping reviews** and the **Arksey and O'Malley (2005)** framework as enhanced by Levac et al. (2010) and the JBI approach (Peters et al., 2020). The review will:

1. Identify the research question
2. Identify relevant studies
3. Study selection
4. Chart the data
5. Collate, summarize, and report results
6. Consult with stakeholders (optional but recommended)

### 6.2 Reporting
The review will be reported according to the **PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR)** (Tricco et al., 2018).

## 9. Ethical Considerations

As a scoping review of published literature, this study does not require IRB approval. However:
- All data will be handled confidentially
- No individual participant data will be identifiable
- Findings will be reported at the aggregate level
- The review will be sensitive to the traumatic nature of the subject matter
