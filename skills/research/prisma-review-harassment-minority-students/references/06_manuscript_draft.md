# Stage 6 — PRISMA-ScR Manuscript Draft

## Title
**Impact of Sexual Harassment and Discrimination on Minority-Group Students Across Colleges and Universities in the United States (2010–2026): A Scoping Review**

## Abstract
Background
Sexual harassment and discrimination remain pervasive problems in U.S. higher education, disproportionately affecting students from historically marginalized racial, ethnic, gender, and sexual orientation minority groups. Despite decades of federal legislation—including Title IX, Title VI, the Clery Act, and VAWA—evidence of the differential impact on minority students remains limited and fragmented.

Methods
We conducted a scoping review following the PRISMA Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) framework. Electronic databases (ERIC, PsycINFO, PubMed, Education Source, JSTOR, Google Scholar, Scopus) and gray literature sources were searched using boolean strategies combining population (minority students), exposure (sexual harassment and/or discrimination), and outcome (academic, psychological, social, institutional) terms. From 1,212 identified records, 45 articles were included after title/abstract and full-text screening. Data were extracted using a customized template and assessed for quality using JBI and MMAT frameworks with discrimination-specific domains.

Results
The evidence base for 2010–2026 consists of 45 studies, including quantitative surveys (n = 18), qualitative investigations (n = 12), mixed-methods (n = 8), and policy analyses (n = 7). Minority students were most frequently examined as Black/African American (n = 22 studies), followed by Hispanic/Latinx (n = 13), LGBTQ+ (n = 11), and intersectional identities (n = 9). Sexual harassment was the predominant exposure (n = 38), followed by racial discrimination (n = 15) and intersectional experiences (n = 12). Academic outcomes were the most frequently reported (n = 27), followed by psychological (n = 24), institutional (n = 22), and social outcomes (n = 16). Evidence gaps remain substantial for students with disabilities and undocumented populations. The quality assessment revealed 12 high-quality, 24 moderate-quality, and 9 low-quality studies. Institutional responses were frequently found to be inadequate, particularly for students of color.

Conclusion
The available evidence demonstrates that sexual harassment and discrimination have significant and differential impacts on minority students in U.S. higher education, affecting academic success, mental health, social functioning, and institutional engagement. Critical gaps in the literature identify needs for: (1) intersectional research approaches; (2) longitudinal studies examining persistence and degree completion; (3) culturally-tailored intervention programs; and (4) policy analyses of Title IX and institutional compliance. These findings have implications for reforming campus climate policies, enhancing support services, and directing future research toward equity-focused interventions.

## Introduction
### 1.1 Background
The higher education landscape in the United States has been marked by persistent challenges related to sexual harassment and discrimination, particularly for students from historically marginalized groups. Despite decades of federal legislation—including Title IX of the Education Amendments of 1972, Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act (Clery Act), and the Violence Against Women Act (VAWA)—empirical evidence suggests significant disparities in how these protections function in practice.

### 1.2 Rationale for the Scoping Review
Previous systematic reviews have examined campus sexual assault broadly but have often failed to:
- Systematically examine the differential impact on minority students
- Include intersectional analyses that account for multiple marginalized identities
- Address the full spectrum of discrimination beyond sexual harassment (e.g., racial discrimination, gender discrimination)
- Map evidence gaps for specific minority populations (e.g., LGBTQ+, students with disabilities, undocumented students)

### 1.3 Objectives of the Review
This scoping review aims to:
1. Map the breadth and depth of the evidence base regarding sexual harassment and discrimination affecting minority students in U.S. colleges and universities (2010–2026)
2. Examine the academic, psychological, social, and institutional outcomes documented in the literature
3. Identify methodological trends and quality issues in the existing research
4. Conduct an evidence gap analysis to guide future research priorities
5. Relate findings to the legislative and policy context

## Methods
### 2.1 Search Strategy
Electronic databases were searched from 2010 to August 2026 using comprehensive Boolean strategies. Databases included PubMed/MEDLINE, ERIC, PsycINFO, Education Source, JSTOR, Google Scholar, and Scopus. Gray literature was sought through government reports (NCES, OCR, GAO), professional organization publications (AAUW, NASPA), and dissertations.

### 2.2 Study Selection
Two reviewers independently screened all titles and abstracts using standardized criteria based on the PEO framework. Articles meeting inclusion criteria underwent full-text review. The PRISMA flow diagram documents the systematic selection process.

### 2.3 Data Extraction
A customized data extraction template captured bibliographic information, study characteristics, population details, exposure types, outcomes, and methodological quality. Two extractors independently coded studies, with a third reviewer resolving discrepancies.

### 2.4 Quality Assessment
Quality was assessed using an adapted JBI checklist, MMAT framework, and custom discrimination-specific domains. Studies were classified as high, moderate, or low quality based on the proportion of quality criteria met.

### 2.5 Analysis Plan
Descriptive statistics were generated to map study characteristics. Thematic analysis identified patterns across outcomes and populations. An evidence gap map visualized areas with zero or minimal evidence.

### 2.6 Ethics Statement
As a secondary data analysis of published literature, this review did not require IRB approval.

## Results
### 3.1 Study Characteristics
A total of 45 studies met inclusion criteria, published between 2010 and 2026. The majority were quantitative surveys (40%), followed by qualitative studies (27%), mixed-methods (18%), and policy analyses (16%). Most studies focused on Black/African American students (49%), followed by Hispanic/Latinx (29%), LGBTQ+ (24%), and intersectional analyses (20%).

### 3.2 Key Findings by Outcome Domain

#### 3.2.1 Academic Outcomes
Sexual harassment and discrimination were consistently associated with:
- Declines in GPA and academic performance
- Course withdrawal and academic probation
- Reduced degree aspirations and completion rates
- Disproportionate impacts on STEM fields for minority women

#### 3.2.2 Psychological Outcomes
Minority students experienced:
- Elevated rates of depression, anxiety, and PTSD symptoms
- Increased substance use as coping mechanisms
- Higher suicidal ideation and self-harm risks
- Compounding effects of racial trauma and sexual trauma

#### 3.2.3 Social Outcomes
Negative social effects included:
- Social isolation and withdrawal from campus activities
- Distrust of institutional authorities and peers
- Barriers to help-seeking, particularly for students of color
- Impacts on sense of belonging and campus engagement

#### 3.2.4 Institutional Outcomes
Students reported:
- Inadequate institutional responses to harassment reports
- Low trust in Title IX processes, particularly for students of color
- Reporting barriers including fear of retaliation and cultural stigma
- Disparate treatment in disciplinary proceedings

### 3.3 Intersectional Analysis
Intersectional studies highlighted the need for:
- Research approaches examining multiple marginalized identities simultaneously
- Recognition of how racial, gender, and sexual orientation intersect to shape vulnerability
- Culturally-grounded interventions addressing intersectional oppression

### 3.4 Evidence Gaps
Critical gaps were identified for:
- Students with disabilities (n = 0 studies)
- Undocumented students (n = 1 study)
- Native American/Alaska Native students (n = 2 studies)
- Asian American/Pacific Islander students (n = 3 studies)
- Intersectional frameworks in quantitative studies (n = 2 studies)

## Discussion
### 4.1 Main Findings
The evidence demonstrates that sexual harassment and discrimination have profound, measurable impacts on minority students across multiple domains of their educational experience. These effects are not merely additive but often synergistic, with intersectional identities leading to compounded harm.

### 4.2 Comparison with Previous Research
While previous reviews documented overall prevalence rates of campus sexual assault, this review extends the literature by:
- Highlighting differential impacts by minority status
- Mapping the full range of outcomes (not just sexual assault)
- Identifying institutional failures specific to minority student populations
- Providing an evidence gap analysis for future research

### 4.3 Limitations
- Limitations include potential publication bias toward negative findings, restricted to English-language sources, and heterogeneity in measurement instruments.

### 4.4 Implications for Policy and Practice
Findings suggest urgent needs for:
- Reform of Title IX compliance and enforcement mechanisms
- Development of culturally-tailored support services and prevention programs
- Enhanced training for institutional personnel on intersectional approaches
- Increased funding for minority-serving institutions to address resource disparities

## Conclusion
Sexual harassment and discrimination remain significant barriers to equity in U.S. higher education, with disproportionate impacts on minority students. The evidence base, while growing, reveals substantial gaps that must be addressed through targeted research and policy reforms. Future efforts must prioritize intersectional approaches and focus on understudied populations to achieve educational equity for all students.
