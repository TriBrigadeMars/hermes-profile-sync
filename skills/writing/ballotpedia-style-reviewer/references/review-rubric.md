# Editorial Review Rubric

Use this rubric to keep reviews consistent and actionable.

## Priority order

1. Neutrality and attribution
2. Structural balance and due weight
3. Prose and diction
4. Ballotpedia-specific usage
5. Mechanics and formatting

## Issue record

For each material issue, capture:

- **Location:** paragraph, sentence, heading, line number, or quoted phrase.
- **Severity:** `MUST FIX`, `SHOULD FIX`, `NEEDS CONTEXT`, or `OPTIONAL`.
- **Dimension:** Credibility, Consistency, Clarity, Cachet, or Structure.
- **Rule/category:** exact Ballotpedia entry or bias category when possible.
- **Why:** concise explanation of the editorial risk.
- **Recommendation:** a concrete change, reporting step, or source check.
- **Suggested wording:** only when the issue can be fixed without inventing facts.

## What a good review avoids

- Generic comments such as `make this more neutral` without identifying the wording or reporting action.
- A numeric `bias score` or `style score`.
- Treating all ideological labels as inherently forbidden; the guide allows some labels with context and requires specific treatment for others.
- Treating every passive construction as an error.
- Rewriting direct quotations to house style.
- Declaring source-selection, story-selection, or photo-selection bias resolved without the relevant sources/corpus/images.
- Adding an opposing claim merely to create symmetry when the evidence does not establish it as a significant viewpoint.

## Suggested overall statuses

Use a qualitative conclusion when useful:

- **Minor style revision** — mechanics/prose dominate; no major neutrality concern identified from the supplied text.
- **Substantive editorial revision** — one or more high-impact neutrality, attribution, diction, or structure problems need correction.
- **Editorial review required** — the draft cannot be confidently assessed without source, legal, or contextual information.

These are workflow labels, not scores or certifications.
