# Source Hierarchy and Interpretation

This skill is built from three user-supplied source snapshots dated August 26, 2026.

## 1. Ballotpedia's Style Guide — controlling source

Use this source first for Ballotpedia-specific language, spelling, capitalization, punctuation, formatting, political terminology, titles, quotations, footnotes, and editorial usage. The guide describes itself as Ballotpedia's authoritative reference for editorial style and usage and organizes its approach around **credibility, consistency, clarity, and cachet**.

If a general journalism convention conflicts with a specific Ballotpedia entry, follow Ballotpedia.

## 2. Ballotpedia: How we categorize bias — controlling neutrality taxonomy

Use this source to name and explain neutrality risks in Ballotpedia's own vocabulary. Its taxonomy contains 25 categories, including editorializing, factive verbs, loaded language, failure to capture competing narratives, speech-tribe terminology, source-selection bias, placement bias, and weasel words.

The taxonomy is diagnostic. Do not infer that every flagged phrase proves the entire article is biased.

## 3. Wikipedia: Neutral point of view — complementary only

Use this source to strengthen analysis of:
- due and undue weight;
- proportional representation of significant viewpoints;
- false balance;
- attribution of opinions and contested assertions;
- impartial tone;
- article structure and section organization;
- source selection and viewpoint prominence.

Do not use Wikipedia terminology to override an explicit Ballotpedia preference.

## Conflict rule

When two sources point in different directions:

1. Apply the explicit Ballotpedia Style Guide rule.
2. Apply the Ballotpedia bias taxonomy to assess neutrality consequences.
3. Use Wikipedia NPOV only to illuminate unresolved structural or due-weight questions.

## Review boundary

A style review evaluates the submitted language and structure. It is not automatically a fact check. Claims about source-selection bias, cherry-picking, omitted narratives, or story selection may require source material beyond the text itself. Mark those issues `NEEDS CONTEXT` when evidence is insufficient.
