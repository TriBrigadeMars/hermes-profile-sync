# Ballotpedia Style Guide — High-Value Quick Reference

This digest is for fast review. For exact wording and edge cases, search `ballotpedia-style-guide-full.txt`.

## Guiding principles

Ballotpedia frames its style around **credibility, consistency, clarity, and cachet**. It prioritizes neutral, fact-based content; uniform style; straightforward accessible prose; and polished presentation.

## Prose and attribution

- Prefer active voice when it improves clarity and identifies a relevant actor; passive voice is acceptable when the actor is unimportant, unknown, or already understood.
- Factive verbs can presuppose a disputed claim. Use neutral attribution (`said`, `stated`, `wrote`) for opinions or narratives when appropriate.
- Avoid scare quotes and never use `so-called`.
- Avoid vague weasel wording; specify the source and evidence.
- Do not alter direct quotations merely to conform them to Ballotpedia style.
- Use full quotations when practical; ellipses must not change the speaker's meaning.

## Lists and structure

- Bulleted lists must be consistent in capitalization/punctuation and parallel in grammar.
- BP News should put the lede in the first paragraph, ideally the first sentence.
- Avoid relative dates such as `yesterday`, `tomorrow`, `today`, or `tonight`; give specific dates.

## Numbers and dates

- In general, spell out numbers zero through nine and use numerals for 10 and above, subject to the guide's listed exceptions.
- Spell out a number at the beginning of a sentence; years may remain numerals and are generally better moved away from sentence-initial position when possible.
- Use `%` with a numeral, with no space.
- Use `percentage points` for changes/differences in percentages.
- Dates use `Month Day, Year` and never ordinal suffixes (`1st`, `2nd`).
- Do not abbreviate months in general article text. BP News has its own month-abbreviation rules.
- Use `more than`/`less than` rather than `over`/`under` for inexact numeric quantities in BP News.

## Capitalization and political titles

- Proper nouns are capitalized; common nouns are lowercased.
- Political titles are capitalized only when directly preceding an official's name; otherwise lowercase them.
- Occupational titles such as `chief of staff`, `campaign manager`, and `press secretary` are generally lowercase even before a name.
- Official governing-body names are capitalized; generic or shortened references are often lowercase.
- Capitalize official political parties (`Democratic`, `Republican`); lowercase general ideologies such as `conservative` and `liberal` unless a proper-noun rule applies.
- `independent` and `nonpartisan` are generally lowercase in article prose.

## Political affiliations

- Parenthetical party affiliations follow Ballotpedia's specific formats. Congress uses party + AP state abbreviation, e.g. `(R-Fla.)`.
- Do not infer party affiliation from indirect association; provide context when the relationship is less direct.

## Common preferred forms

- `advisor` (except when an official title uses another spelling)
- `amid`, not `amidst`
- `bipartisan`, not `bi-partisan`
- `email`, not `e-mail`
- `healthcare` as one word
- `judgment`, not `judgement`
- `midterm election`, not `mid-term election`
- `nationwide`, `statewide`
- `noncitizen`, `nonpartisan`, `nonprofit`
- `re-elect`, `re-election`
- `front-runner`
- `runoff`
- `toward`, not `towards`
- `comprised of` should normally be replaced with `composed of`, `made up of`, or a correct `comprises` construction.

## Terminology requiring special care

The guide contains topic-specific rules that must be checked directly for political and socially contested subjects. High-impact examples include:

### Abortion
Use specific descriptions of actual policy positions. Avoid unqualified speech-tribe terms such as `pro-life`, `pro-choice`, `anti-abortion`, `abortion rights`, and similar shorthand in Ballotpedia's own voice. Attribute source terminology when relevant. The guide prefers `abortion in the third trimester` to `late-term abortion` and contains additional topic-specific language rules.

### Immigration
The guide uses `individual(s) residing in the United States without legal permission` for the described category and identifies several common terms as speech-tribe terminology. `Illegal immigration` is acceptable; `illegal immigrant` is not.

### Firearms
Use `firearms` or `guns`; avoid several contested shorthand terms such as `gun control`, `gun rights`, and `constitutional carry` in Ballotpedia's own voice. Describe the actual policy instead.

### Ideological labels
Do not use `left-wing`, `leftist`, `far-left`, `right-wing`, `rightist`, or `far-right` to characterize a person's ideology. Describe affiliation, voting history, and issue positions.

### Race and ethnicity
The guide has specific entries for Black, White, African American, Asian American, Arab American, Hispanic, Latino/Latina, Native Americans, dual heritage, and related terms. Be specific and follow known individual preferences where the guide directs.

### Sexuality and gender identity
Only include sexuality/gender identity when pertinent. The guide contains specific entries for transgender terminology, pronouns, name changes, sports participation, and same-sex marriage. Search the exact entry before recommending wording.

### Climate and energy
Be specific about policies and energy sources. Several common phrases are treated as speech-tribe or borderline terms and require attribution or more descriptive alternatives.

## Quotations

- Periods and commas go inside quotation marks.
- Do not use quotation marks around a few ordinary words merely because a source used them.
- Use brackets for clarifications inside quotations under the guide's rules.
- Do not make stylistic changes inside quotations to match house style.
- Avoid split quotes when practical.
- Scare quotes should be removed or replaced with a real quotation/paraphrase.

## Footnotes

The guide contains detailed templates for journal articles, books, campaign websites, court cases, interviews/correspondence, social media, websites, and YouTube. For footnote review, always search the full extracted guide rather than relying only on this digest.
