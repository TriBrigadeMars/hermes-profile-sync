# Complementary Neutrality Principles

This reference distills the supplied **Wikipedia: Neutral point of view** document for use only as a complement to Ballotpedia's own rules.

## Core distinctions

- Do not state opinions as facts.
- Do not state seriously contested assertions as settled facts; attribute them to the relevant source or viewpoint.
- Do not turn established facts into mere opinions when reliable sourcing supports direct factual treatment.
- Prefer nonjudgmental language.
- Give significant viewpoints prominence in proportion to their representation in reliable sources.

## Due weight, not equal weight

Neutrality does not require a 50/50 split among all positions. A minority view should not automatically receive the same space or prominence as a majority or well-established view. Conversely, a significant competing view should not be omitted simply because the writer disagrees with it.

When assessing due weight, look at the prominence of viewpoints in reliable published sourcing, not merely the number of advocates quoted in one draft.

## Structural neutrality

Article structure can itself create bias. Review:
- section order;
- headline or lede emphasis;
- whether controversy is isolated into adversarial `pro` and `con` sections rather than integrated into the narrative;
- whether one viewpoint receives a long standalone treatment while others are compressed or buried;
- whether background material implies a hierarchy of credibility unsupported by the source record.

Avoid creating an article that reads like a back-and-forth debate when a factual narrative can present disagreements more clearly.

## False balance

Do not include fringe or tiny-minority positions solely to create symmetry. Giving an unsupported or extremely marginal view equal standing can itself mislead readers about its real-world prominence.

## Attribution and biased statements

For opinions and contentious characterizations:
- identify who holds the view;
- use in-text attribution rather than vague phrases such as `some people say`;
- distinguish the source's judgment from the publication's voice;
- substantiate the statement with sourcing when source materials are part of the review.

## Impartial tone

Describe disputes rather than participating in them. The article's voice should neither endorse nor reject a viewpoint through praise, ridicule, sarcasm, loaded descriptors, or selective presentation.

## Source selection

Prefer reliable sources appropriate to the claim. A source's perspective does not automatically disqualify it, but the overall source set and article framing should not merely reproduce one side's perspective. This principle complements Ballotpedia's `source selection bias` and `parroting the news` categories.
