# Source Principles: Smart Brevity

Source: *Smart Brevity: The Power of Saying More with Less* by Jim VandeHei, Mike Allen, and Roy Schwartz (2022).

This file contains distilled operational principles, not reproduced book text.

Applied principles:
- audience-first communication;
- short but not shallow;
- identify the most important point and surface it early;
- explain why information matters;
- strong, active, concrete wording;
- plain human language over inflated business jargon;
- selection and hierarchy are part of good editing;
- readers scan digital material, so structure for efficient consumption;
- cut repeated context and low-value words without cutting needed nuance.

Application to professional reframing:
- business-legible does not mean jargon-heavy;
- LinkedIn copy should put the highest-value supported fact/function first;
- shorter wording is better only when it preserves meaning;
- remove corporate phrasing that makes a simple function harder to understand.
