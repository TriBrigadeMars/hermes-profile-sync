# Impact Framing

## Prefer purpose and consequence over decorative adjectives

Weak:
"Successfully performed high-quality monthly reporting."

Better:
"Produced monthly operational reports for department leaders."

Stronger only if supported:
"Produced monthly operational reports used by department leaders to allocate staffing."

## Impact ladder

1. **Activity:** what was done.
2. **Purpose:** what business need it served.
3. **Immediate result:** what changed directly.
4. **Operational outcome:** what improved for a team/process/customer.
5. **Organizational outcome:** larger effect, only if evidenced.

Use the highest supported rung, not the highest imaginable rung.

## Causality

Use `resulting in`, `driving`, `increasing`, `reducing`, or `saving` only when evidence supports the causal relationship.

When causality is uncertain, prefer:
- supported;
- contributed to;
- helped enable;
- informed;
- was used to;
- supported the team's effort to.
